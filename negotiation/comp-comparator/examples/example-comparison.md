# Example: Comp Comparison — Fictional Offer

*This is a fictional worked example showing what the comp-comparator skill
produces. Names, companies, and numbers are illustrative only.*

---

## Inputs used for this example

- **Target comp:** Base $160,000-$175,000, bonus target 10%, no equity
  expectation, standard benefits
- **Offer received:** Riverside Health Partners, Senior Program Manager
- **Offer letter excerpt:** *"Employee will be eligible to participate in the
  Company's annual incentive program, target amount to be determined annually
  by the Compensation Committee. The Company reserves the right to modify or
  eliminate incentive programs at its discretion."*
- **Verbal context:** Recruiter stated "the bonus is typically around 8-10% of
  base" during the offer call, but this is not in the written offer.

---

## 1. Component breakdown

| Component | Your Target | Riverside Offer |
|---|---|---|
| Base | $160,000-$175,000 | $152,000 |
| Sign-on bonus | n/a | None offered |
| Performance bonus | 10% target, guaranteed structure | **Unconfirmed** — "eligible to participate," no stated target in writing |
| Equity | n/a | None (mutual company structure, not applicable) |
| PTO | 20 days | 18 days |
| Start date | Flexible | Proposed in 3 weeks |

## 2. Total comp estimate

**Guaranteed total comp:** $152,000 (base only)

**Potential upside (not guaranteed):** +$12,160-$15,200 if the verbally-stated
8-10% bonus materializes at target. This is **not included** in the
guaranteed total because the written offer does not commit to a bonus amount,
and the company has explicit discretion to modify or eliminate the program.

This offer is below your stated base target range of $160,000-$175,000.

## 3. Language risk flags

**Flag 1 — Bonus is verbal-only, not written.**
The recruiter stated 8-10% verbally. The offer letter only says you're
"eligible to participate" in a program whose target is "to be determined
annually" by a committee, with an explicit right to modify or eliminate it.
**Practical risk:** if the program is reduced or eliminated in a future year,
you have no written commitment to point to. The verbal 8-10% figure carries
no contractual weight.

**Flag 2 — "Reserves the right to modify or eliminate" is broad.**
This language isn't unusual, but combined with an unconfirmed bonus target,
it means the entire bonus component of your comp is currently undefined and
revocable. This is the highest-priority item to address before signing.

## 4. Negotiation sequencing plan

1. **Base salary first.** Your base ($152,000) is below your stated range.
   This is the most flexible lever for most employers and should be addressed
   before anything else. Ask: "Is there flexibility on the base salary? My
   range based on my experience and the market for this role is
   $160,000-$175,000."
   - *Soft no:* "Let me check with finance and get back to you" → there's
     room, follow up.
   - *Hard no:* "This is the top of our band for this level" → may be final;
     consider whether other components can close the gap.

2. **Written bonus confirmation, second.** Regardless of the base outcome,
   ask for the verbally-discussed bonus target to be reflected in writing.
   Frame it as routine: "Could we get the bonus target the team discussed
   (8-10%) reflected in the offer letter, even as a target rather than a
   guarantee? I just want to make sure I'm working from accurate numbers."
   - This doesn't need to be adversarial. A reasonable employer will often
     add a sentence stating the *current target percentage* even if they
     can't guarantee it won't change in future years. That's meaningfully
     better than no number at all.

3. **PTO, third.** Smaller ask, often easier to get than base or bonus
   changes. "Would it be possible to match my current 20 days of PTO?"

4. **Start date, last.** Use this as a low-cost concession point if needed —
   moving your start date by a week or two often costs the employer nothing
   and can be traded for flexibility elsewhere.

**Not applicable:** Equity. Riverside is structured such that equity isn't
part of comp for this role; raising it would signal you didn't research the
company's structure.

## 5. Negotiation principles to remember

- Don't state your current or expected salary first if asked; redirect:
  "I'd rather understand the range you're working with for this role first."
- Treat the written offer as the source of truth. A verbal number that
  doesn't appear in writing is not part of your comp until it does.
- Asking for a number to be reflected in writing is a normal, low-friction
  request. Most recruiters have done this before and won't read it as
  distrustful if framed as "for my records."
- Sequence matters. Asking for equity at a company structurally unable to
  offer it, or asking for PTO before addressing a below-range base, wastes
  your highest-leverage moment (right after the offer, before you've
  accepted) on a lower-impact item.
