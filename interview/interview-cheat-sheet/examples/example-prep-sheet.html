Certain Tell Me About Yourself ~90 sec · First impression · Don't rush it
Anchors NineYears→ TwelveTeams→ 75to98→ WhyNorthwind
01Who I Am~10 sec

"I'm a Technical Program Manager with nine years of experience leading delivery across complex, cross-functional engineering organizations. [PAUSE]"

Keep it short. One sentence, then move to the numbers.
02The Numbers~30 sec

"Most recently I led release governance across twelve engineering teams in a regulated environment. [PAUSE] I built the program tracking infrastructure from scratch and took on-time delivery from 75% to 98%. [SLOW] We moved from an unpredictable 8 to 9 week release cycle to a strict six-week cadence, and recovered roughly $1M in engineering capacity in a year by cutting hotfix volume."

03Why Northwind~15 sec

"What draws me to Northwind is the bridge this role sits on — between research-stage controls work and production software. [PAUSE] That coordination problem, getting research and production teams moving on the same timeline, [SLOW] is exactly the kind of problem I've built my career around."

Stop after "built my career around." Let them respond.
Certain Why Did You Leave Your Last Role? Keep it brief. Don't elaborate unless pushed.
"My role was eliminated as part of a broader restructuring, it wasn't performance-related. [PAUSE] I'd been there for a while and was ready for a new challenge regardless, so the timing worked out, honestly."
Certain Salary Expectations
"I'm looking at total comp in the range posted for this role, and I'd want to understand the full package, base, bonus, and equity, before getting more specific. [PAUSE] What's the range you're working with for this position?"
Don't name a number first if you can avoid it. Redirect to their range.
Likely Tell Me About a Time You Managed a Complex Cross-Team Dependency JD emphasizes coordinating research and production teams
Anchors WrongAssumption→ EscalatedSameDay→ ResetTimeline→ Delivered
01Platform Migration — Architecture Miss~75 sec

"During a major platform integration, I was responsible for a regulatory reporting feature that depended on a separate infrastructure team's data layer. [PAUSE] Midway through, I caught that our original architecture assumption was wrong, the team had assumed we could capture the data at the adapter layer, but it actually needed to be aggregated and stored centrally on the platform. That's a significant infrastructure change that was never in scope. [SLOW] I escalated to leadership the same day, took ownership of the miss, and we reset the timeline together with the infrastructure team. [PAUSE] We delivered on the revised date and met the compliance deadline in full."

If they ask for a second story — Release Cadence Change
Anchors SkepticalSVP→ DataDriven→ 98Percent
"Separately, I influenced a skeptical VP of Engineering to move our whole release cadence to a strict six-week cycle, using delivery data rather than a top-down mandate. [PAUSE] On-time delivery went from 75% to 98% within two quarters, [SLOW] and the teams kept the new cadence after I moved on, which told me the change had actually stuck."
Note: This story also works as your "influence without authority" answer. Don't use it twice in the same conversation.
Possible How Familiar Are You With Our Controls Stack / Middleware? Don't fake it. Honest framing wins more trust than overclaiming.
"I haven't worked hands-on with robotics middleware or controls stacks specifically. [PAUSE] Where I'm coming in is on the program execution side, release governance, cross-team dependency management, and delivery predictability for complex technical programs. [SLOW] I've been getting up to speed on how your stack fits together so I can ask informed questions in technical discussions, but I want to be upfront that my depth there is foundational, not expert."
If pushed for more depth: "I'm comfortable discussing system-level tradeoffs, like sequencing dependencies between research and production timelines, but I'd defer to your engineers on the specific middleware tradeoffs. That's a gap I'd close quickly, but I won't pretend it's not a gap today."
Close Questions to Ask Them Pick 2. Always close with next steps.
01
Priority ask: "How does this role currently split time between the research-stage controls teams and the production software teams? Is that balance something you expect to shift?"
Surfaces the real scope of the role and shows you read the JD's framing carefully.
02
"What does release predictability look like today, is there an existing cadence, or is this role expected to help establish one?"
Tells you whether you'd be improving an existing system or building one, which changes how you'd frame your first 90 days.
03
Always close with this: "What are the next steps after this conversation, and what's the timeline looking like?"
Never leave without knowing the path forward.
